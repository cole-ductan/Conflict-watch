CIA Network Breached

US CIA, ODNI suffer cybersecurity breach of some unclassified information, NYT reports

mediumVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Cyber / CIA & ODNI · Tuesday, November 24, 2026 at 1:52 PM CT

In plain English

Hackers broke into a computer network the CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence use to study publicly available information, exposing some unclassified data, the New York Times reported Tuesday. Officials say no classified information was affected, and no one has said who did it or exactly what was taken.

Full brief

The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence recently discovered a breach of a network used to look for intelligence insights in public, unclassified material, the New York Times reported Tuesday, citing people familiar with the matter. CNN confirmed the intrusion with four sources, and the Associated Press confirmed it with a person familiar with the incident, who said the issue has been resolved. The network handles open-source intelligence — the collection and analysis of publicly available data from the internet and commercial databases. An ODNI official said there have been 'no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems,' and the CIA declined to comment. It is not clear who was responsible or exactly what unclassified material was exposed; the Times reported information held by other government agencies was also believed to have been exposed. Two CNN sources said the incident does not appear related to the apparent hack of the FBI's National Crime Information Center database reported Friday. It is the latest in a string of federal cybersecurity incidents, which also hit the Agriculture Department's payment systems, and it comes as DNI Jay Clayton serves as interim national cyber director. The incidents have coincided with unrelated high-profile tech outages affecting banks, the National Security Agency and the FAA's air-traffic control system. Contested / unverified: who is responsible (no attribution has been made), exactly what data was exposed (not disclosed), and whether other agencies' data was exposed (reported as believed, not confirmed).

Why this matters for the US

the breach reached the US intelligence community's own networks — even unclassified systems carry sensitive analytical work and other agencies' data — and it lands amid a run of intrusions into federal systems, showing US government networks remain exposed.
