Russian Ship Ablaze

Russian cargo ship hit by drones in Baltic, Finland tightens security

mediumVerifiedRussia–Ukraine · Maritime attack — Baltic Sea · Saturday, November 21, 2026 at 12:50 AM CT

In plain English

A Russian cargo ship linked to Russia's military caught fire in the Baltic Sea on Saturday after being hit by two drones. The 16 crew members got off safely, and the burning ship drifted into Finnish waters. Hours later, a Russian helicopter briefly flew into Finnish airspace near the same area. Finland says Ukraine was likely behind the drone strike, but has no proof yet.

Full brief

What happened

The Sparta II is a 121-metre Russian-flagged roll-on/roll-off cargo vessel owned by Oboronlogistika, a company that moves supplies for the Russian defence ministry; the vessel is under US sanctions, according to the US Treasury Department. It left St Petersburg on Friday bound for Kaliningrad. Shortly before 9 a.m. local time on Saturday, Nov 21 (about 12:50 AM CT, 06:50 UTC), two drones struck the ship in the eastern Gulf of Finland, west of Gogland Island (Suursaari), setting it on fire. The 16-member crew evacuated to lifeboats and was taken aboard a nearby Russian ship; no casualties were reported. The burning vessel drifted into Finnish waters and was anchored off Loviisa, with firefighting continuing into Saturday night — Finnish authorities warned the fire could burn for days and trigger further explosions. Finland's Defence Forces detected the attack on Saturday morning. The Finnish Border Guard is leading the rescue operation and is in contact with Russian maritime rescue authorities. Finland imposed temporary air and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland and immediately informed NATO of the strike. Hours later, at 12:34 p.m. local time (4:34 AM CT), a Russian helicopter was suspected of entering Finnish airspace off the coast of Loviisa, penetrating 4.1 km (2.5 miles) into Finnish territory. Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen said the suspected violation was possibly related to rescue or monitoring operations for the damaged ship; the Finnish Border Guard is investigating.

Contested claims

Attribution: Finnish armed forces spokesperson Aki-Mauri Huhtinen said it was “likely that Ukraine is behind” the attack, adding there was no conclusive evidence and it was unclear why this ship was targeted; Ukraine has not claimed responsibility. Location: Finland's Defence Ministry says the strike happened in international waters west of Gogland Island, while Border Guard commander Ilja Iljin said the ship was hit in Russian territorial waters.

Why this matters for the US

The ship is a US-sanctioned Russian military logistics vessel, and the strike left it burning in a NATO ally's waters; a Russian helicopter then violated that same ally's airspace — the second Russian airspace violation of a NATO country in two days, after Friday's missiles landed in Poland. It raises the risk of a NATO-Russia miscalculation that could pull the US in.

⚠ Attribution is Finland's assessment only — Ukraine “likely” behind the strike, no conclusive evidence, and Ukraine has not claimed it; the strike location is contested between Finnish authorities.
Confidence

High for the core facts — the drone strike, the fire, the crew's safe evacuation, the drift into Finnish waters and the helicopter violation (Reuters wire, Finnish Defence Ministry statement, Finnish Border Guard and CNN all agree). Medium on attribution: Finland says Ukraine is “likely” behind the strike, with no conclusive evidence, and Ukraine has not claimed it. Contested: where the strike happened — Finland's Defence Ministry says international waters west of Gogland Island; Border Guard commander Ilja Iljin said the ship was hit in Russian territorial waters.
