China Pressures Taiwan Vote

Chinese warplanes and warships stage a joint patrol around Taiwan on the eve of its local elections

mediumVerifiedChina–Taiwan · Joint patrol — Taiwan Strait · Thursday, November 26, 2026 at 11:10 PM CT

In plain English

Taiwan's defence ministry said China sent 30 military aircraft around the island on Friday, the day before Taiwan's local elections, and 26 of them crossed the median line of the Taiwan Strait. The aircraft — fighters, bombers, early-warning planes and drones — flew in four sectors around Taiwan together with Chinese warships, in what Beijing calls a joint combat readiness patrol. Taiwan called it harassment of the air and sea around the island. No shots were fired, and no aircraft entered Taiwan's territorial airspace.

Full brief

What happened

Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense said on Friday, November 27, that China had sent 30 military aircraft sorties out over the sea from 9:25 a.m. Taipei time, and that 26 of them crossed the median line of the Taiwan Strait or its extension, entering Taiwan's northern, central, south-western and eastern airspace. The aircraft operated together with Chinese warships in what Beijing calls a joint combat readiness patrol. The ministry's press release, issued at 1:10 p.m. Taipei time, listed J-10 and J-16 fighters, Su-30 fighters, H-6K bombers and KJ-500 airborne early warning aircraft, together with drones, among the aircraft involved. The ministry described the activity as harassment of the air and sea space around the island. Taiwan's military said it was monitoring the activity and responding with its own aircraft, naval vessels and missile systems.

Context

The patrol came the day before Taiwan's nine-in-one local elections on Saturday, November 28. A week earlier, on November 20, the ministry reported 21 sorties with 15 crossing the median line, entering only the central and south-western sectors; this time the aircraft reached four sectors and 11 more crossed the line. This is a separate event from the Dacheng Bay amphibious landing drill already on this dashboard, and from the ministry's routine daily counts.

Contested claims

A social-media account has claimed some of the Chinese aircraft broadcast the '1400' transponder code to impersonate US military aircraft; that claim is not in the defence ministry's release and has not been independently confirmed, so it is not treated as fact here. The election-pressure reading is context, not a stated Chinese aim: Beijing describes these patrols as joint combat readiness patrols. The median line is an informal line, not a treaty boundary, and crossing it is not an entry into Taiwan's territorial airspace.

Why this matters for the US

Taiwan is the central flashpoint for US forces in the Pacific, and China just showed it can put bombers and early-warning aircraft in four sectors around the island at once, on election eve — a demonstration of how fast it can surround Taiwan, and a test of how the US and its allies respond.

⚠ Patrol, not an attack — no shots fired and no entry into Taiwan's territorial airspace; the median line is an informal line, not a treaty boundary. A social-media claim that PLA aircraft broadcast a US transponder code is unverified and not in the ministry's release.
Confidence

High on the patrol itself — Taiwan's Ministry of National Defence announced it in a press release with aircraft types and numbers, corroborated by Taiwanese media (FTV News, TaiwanPlus) and the specialist outlet Taiwan Defence. The election-pressure reading is context, not a stated Chinese aim, and the transponder-code claim is unverified.
