Tanker Loads Iranian Crude

Oil tanker loads Iranian crude in Gulf, TankerTrackers.com says (Reuters, Nov 19, 2026)

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Blockade evasion — Iranian crude · Thursday, November 19, 2026 at 6:47 AM CT

In plain English

A tanker is loading Iranian crude oil in the Gulf, moving the oil from one ship to another at sea. If it gets out past the US naval blockade, it would be the first tanker to successfully export Iranian crude since the US reimposed the blockade in July. The ship monitoring service that spotted it, TankerTrackers.com, would not name the vessel, but said it is not under US Treasury sanctions — which is how it got into the area without drawing attention. It is not yet clear whether the tanker will make it out or be stopped.

Full brief

What happened

A tanker was loading Iranian crude oil in the Gulf via ship-to-ship transfer on Thursday, Nov 19, TankerTrackers.com told Reuters. If the tanker leaves the blockade perimeter, it would be the first successful export of seaborne Iranian crude since the United States reimposed its naval blockade in mid-July, Samir Madani, co-founder of TankerTrackers.com, told Reuters. Madani declined to identify the vessel but said it was not subject to US Treasury sanctions, allowing it to enter the region without attracting scrutiny.

Context

The US naval blockade on Iran-related oil exports was first imposed April 13, lifted in June after a draft understanding with Tehran, and reimposed July 14 after the agreement collapsed. On Wednesday, Nov 18, CENTCOM widened the blockade to cover exactly this kind of at-sea transfer: any ship receiving cargo via ship-to-ship transfer from a vessel loaded at an Iranian port may be treated as evading the blockade and can be stopped, boarded, diverted, or captured. Visible Iranian crude exports have collapsed to near zero in recent months as a result of the blockade, though Reuters reported in October that Iran has still managed to export some liquefied petroleum gas and jet fuel since July.

Why this matters for the US

This is the first test of the expanded blockade rules — a tanker loading Iranian crude at sea, one day after CENTCOM said such transfers can be treated as blockade evasion. If it slips out, the blockade's grip on Iranian crude is weaker than claimed; if US forces stop it, the new rules get their first enforcement. Either way, the standoff over Gulf shipping is now playing out ship by ship.

⚠ Single-source attribution (TankerTrackers.com via Reuters); vessel unnamed; no confirmation yet that the tanker left the blockade perimeter or was intercepted.
Confidence

High that Reuters reported TankerTrackers.com's finding on Nov 19 (Reuters wire, quoting co-founder Samir Madani by name). The loading itself is TankerTrackers.com's attribution — a single source, and the vessel is unnamed, so the tanker has not been independently identified. Medium that this becomes the first successful crude export since July — that depends on whether the tanker leaves the blockade perimeter, which had not happened when Reuters reported it.
