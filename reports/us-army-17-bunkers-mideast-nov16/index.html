Army Seeks 17 Bunkers

US Army seeks 17 concrete bunkers at Middle East bases within 45 days as Iran threat persists

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · US posture — force protection · Monday, November 16, 2026 at 2:43 PM CT

In plain English

The US Army is moving to build 17 reinforced-concrete bunkers at bases in Israel, Saudi Arabia and Lebanon to protect troops from Iranian missile and drone fire. Ten bunkers are planned for Israel, five for Saudi Arabia and two for Lebanon, replacing shelters made from converted shipping containers that the Army says leave soldiers at risk. The work must be finished within 45 days of a task order. The move follows months of Iranian strikes that have damaged several US bases and killed at least 23 American personnel.

Full brief

What happened

The US Army has posted a Sources Sought notice for 17 reinforced-concrete bunkers at five primary installations: one in Israel, two in Saudi Arabia and two in Lebanon (10 bunkers in Israel, five in Saudi Arabia, two in Lebanon). The bunkers are designed to survive indirect fire — rockets, missiles and drones — using precast concrete sections, steel doors and sandbag cover, and must be completed within 45 days of a task order. They replace personnel shelters made from converted shipping containers. The notice is a survey of industry capacity, not a contract award; responses are due November 25 and the follow-on solicitation is expected to allow only three days for quotes. The Army did not name the specific bases — exact installation names and coordinates will be shared only with eligible contractors.

Context

the notice follows months of Iranian missile and drone strikes that damaged several US bases across the region and killed at least 23 US personnel, including a March 2026 strike on an Army command post in Kuwait that killed six service members. It was reported by Defense News and covered by Army Times and Military Times on November 16.

Why this matters for the US

the Army is hardening shelters for its own troops because it expects them to stay exposed to Iranian fire: a concrete-bunker program on a 45-day clock signals Washington is preparing for a long confrontation rather than a short one.

⚠ Sources Sought notice, not a contract — no bases named; work not yet awarded
Confidence

High — Army Sources Sought notice, reported by Defense News and corroborated by Army Times and Military Times; the notice itself is a capacity survey, not a contract award.
