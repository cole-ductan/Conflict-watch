Iran Blacklists Tugboats

Hormuz blacklists tugboats, pressuring tanker traffic

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Maritime pressure — Strait of Hormuz · Tuesday, November 24, 2026 at 12:16 PM CT

In plain English

Iran's Strait authority has put 26 tugboats on its blacklist for helping tankers that sail through the Strait of Hormuz without Tehran's permission. The list now covers 266 vessels. Tugboats are the boats that tow damaged tankers to safety and fight fires at sea — blacklisting them is Iran's way of squeezing the rescue and salvage help that US-protected tanker traffic depends on.

Full brief

Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA) said Tuesday it added 26 tugboats to its "non-compliance list" for providing services to vessels it considers offenders using the southern route through the Strait of Hormuz, according to the semi-official Tasnim news agency as reported by Bloomberg/Transport Topics. The tugs are registered in several countries, including the UAE, Singapore and India. The authority has now listed 266 vessels in total. The PGSA, established in May 2026, announced the move in a post on X on Nov 24. Maritime risk firm Orbital Risk Intelligence assesses Iran is likely trying to deny towage, firefighting and salvage services to the vessels it attacks.

Context

tugboats have been crucial to keeping traffic moving — towing tankers damaged in Iranian attacks to safety — and several have been damaged in recent months, per the International Maritime Organization: a Panama-flagged tug was damaged earlier this month, a Netherlands-flagged vessel incident injured a seafarer, and in March four seafarers died and three were severely injured in a tug incident off the coast of Oman.

Contested claims

none on the listing itself — the PGSA announced it directly. The "deny salvage" reading is Orbital Risk Intelligence's assessment, attributed to them, not a stated Iranian aim.

Why this matters for the US

US-escorted tankers rely on tugboats for towing, firefighting and salvage when struck; blacklisting the tugs raises the cost and risk of keeping oil moving through Hormuz under US protection, and extends Iran's pressure from the tankers themselves to the safety net behind them.

⚠ No attack and no casualties — this is administrative pressure on the support ecosystem for US-protected shipping: blacklisting the tugs extends Iran's pressure from the tankers themselves to the safety net behind them.
