Ground stop lifted after ~4.5 hours

FAA orders nationwide ground stop for all US airports after telecom outage hits all 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers

lowVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Homeland / Air traffic · Friday, November 13, 2026 at 2:54 PM CT

In plain English

The FAA stopped every flight in the United States on Friday afternoon after a telecom failure hit the centers that guide planes between airports. The stop was lifted at about 7:15 p.m. CT after about 4.5 hours. More than 11,000 flights were delayed and 4,000 canceled. Officials say there is no sign this was an attack — the cause is still being investigated.

Full brief

The FAA's Air Traffic Control System Command Center ordered a nationwide ground stop at 3:54 p.m. ET / 2:54 p.m. CT on Friday, “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” after a communications failure hit the centers that guide planes between airports. The FAA said on X there had been “a temporary telecommunications outage” and “out of an abundance of caution all flights have been halted. Every flight has been accounted for.” The stop was lifted at about 7:15 p.m. CT / 8:15 p.m. ET, after about 4.5 hours, with more than 11,000 flights delayed and 4,000 canceled on Friday (FlightAware, as reported); cancellations continued into Saturday as airlines repositioned aircraft and crews. On Saturday, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the outage likely stemmed from upgrade work on an old telecommunications system: contractor L3Harris was replacing a copper line with fiber at a remote FAA location, a router began flooding other routers on the air-traffic system, and 17 of the 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers were affected, though none “actually went dark,” according to FAA chief Bryan Bedford. Duffy said it is still unclear whether the cause was a contractor error or faulty equipment, and about 30% of the copper lines have yet to be replaced. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there is “no indication… this was a threat to the United States” and “no reason to believe as of right now that this was a nefarious attack.” The cause is under investigation and is not presented here as a cyberattack or sabotage. It is only the third nationwide departure halt in history, after 9/11 and the Jan 2023 NOTAM outage, A separate Microsoft 365 outage the same week is covered in its own report in this archive; no connection between the two has been established.

⚠ Lifted ~7:15 p.m. CT Nov 13 — cause under investigation, no indication of an attack
Confidence

High that the FAA ordered the ground stop and lifted it, and that officials said no attack is indicated (FAA statements; NPR and USA Today on the lift; Duffy/Bedford briefing Nov 14, via CNN); the underlying cause is under investigation and unknown.
