Iran's seven-day peace plan

Iran's seven-day roadmap (Sept 25) — the $12bn plan; since superseded by a two-day plan

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Diplomacy / Roadmap · Friday, September 25, 2026 at 7:00 AM CT

In plain English

Back in September, Iran offered a seven-day plan to end the war: the US would lift its blockade and sanctions and release about $12 billion in frozen Iranian money, and the Strait of Hormuz would reopen on day 6. Trump reportedly rejected it, and Iran has since replaced it with a faster two-day plan.

Full brief

Al Jazeera reported on Sept 25, 2026 that Iran had submitted a seven-day roadmap to end the war and was awaiting Washington's response. Under that plan, Washington would lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports, waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales, release an estimated $12bn in frozen Iranian assets, and observe a regional ceasefire including Lebanon and Yemen; the Strait of Hormuz would reopen on day 6 and talks would start on day 7. The Wall Street Journal reported Trump rejected the plan. Tehran has since superseded the seven-day plan with a two-day plan (FM Abbas Araghchi, Nov 2): the US takes specified steps on day 1, and Iran reopens Hormuz on day 2.

⚠ Corrected Nov 12: date fixed from Nov 11 to Sept 25, 2026
Confidence

High that Al Jazeera reported the plan on Sept 25, 2026 — this report was previously dated Nov 11 in this archive and has been corrected; the $12bn figure belongs to this Sept 25 roadmap (and the June Islamabad MoU), not to any November list. The Trump rejection is WSJ reporting. The two-day successor plan is Araghchi's statement of Nov 2, reported Nov 3.
