Air Force Cloud Hacked

Hackers breach US Air Force flagship Cloud One computing platform

mediumVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Cyber / US Air Force · Friday, November 27, 2026 at 5:00 AM CT

In plain English

Hackers broke into Cloud One, the US Air Force's main cloud computing platform, Reuters reported on Friday, citing three people familiar with the matter. The intrusion was discovered within the past month while engineers were upgrading the system. The hackers reached the platform's control plane — the layer that manages everything running on it — by exploiting a software flaw. Reuters could not establish who the hackers were, how far they got, or whether any data was stolen. The Air Force has paused moving more applications onto the platform, blocked software updates, and called in US Cyber Command to assess the damage.

Full brief

What happened

Hackers breached Cloud One, the US Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters on Friday, November 27, 2026. The intrusion was discovered within the past month or so during work to upgrade the system, and the discovery led to disruption across the platform. One source said the intruders exploited a software vulnerability to reach the control plane, the management layer for the platform, likening it to a universal remote: in theory it could let them check the operational status of military aircraft or the movement of spare parts, though it was unclear whether they did so. Reuters could not establish the identity of the hackers, the full scope of the intrusion, or whether any data had been stolen.

Context

Cloud One, started in 2017, combines Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet cloud services to power scores of Air Force applications, including personnel data and pay, logistics and maintenance. It also hosts Cloud-Based Command and Control, which collates air defence radar and other data feeds from across North America, and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks bombs and missiles at more than 200 US Air Force locations worldwide. Leidos took over as prime contractor in 2025. The discovery has paused efforts to migrate back-office applications onto Cloud One, blocked updates to applications already running there — leaving personnel unable to apply fixes just as a new batch of serious software vulnerabilities was disclosed — and led to some users having administrator rights revoked. US Cyber Command has been brought in to assess the damage. The Air Force told Reuters it 'does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems.'

Contested claims

None: the breach itself is reported by Reuters on the basis of five sources familiar with the matter; attribution, scope and data theft are all explicitly unknown, and this report does not attribute the intrusion to any actor or state.

Why this matters for the US

This is a breach of the cloud that runs the Air Force's air-defence data, munitions tracking, personnel records and logistics — with the intruders reaching the master control layer — and it comes in a string of at least six other recently disclosed US federal cyber incidents, including breaches at the CIA, the FBI, the Pentagon and the Treasury.

⚠ Attribution unknown — no actor or state has been named, and Reuters could not establish whether any data was stolen; do not treat this as a confirmed state-backed attack.
Confidence

High that Cloud One was breached — Reuters reported it on the basis of five people familiar with the matter, and the disruption it describes (paused migrations, blocked updates, revoked admin rights) is corroborated by multiple outlets. Low on attribution, scope and data theft — Reuters explicitly could not establish any of the three, and no official confirmation or denial has been published.
