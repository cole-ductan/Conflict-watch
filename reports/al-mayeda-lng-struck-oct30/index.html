LNG Carrier Struck

Qatar LNG carrier struck by 'unidentified objects' in Gulf (Al Jazeera, Nov 1, 2026)

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Maritime attack — Arabian Gulf · October 30, 2026 · time not stated by the source

In plain English

A Qatari gas carrier, the Al Mayeda, was hit by unidentified objects while sailing in the Gulf on Friday evening, October 30. No one on board was hurt, nothing leaked into the sea, and the ship stayed safe and kept sailing. Qatar called it a dangerous attack on shipping and world energy supplies. No one has said who did it or what hit the ship.

Full brief

What happened

Qatar Gas Transport Company (Nakilat) said its liquefied natural gas carrier Al Mayeda was struck by unidentified objects while transiting the Arabian Gulf on Friday evening, October 30. Nakilat disclosed the incident in a statement published November 1 through the Qatar Stock Exchange. No crew member was injured, there was no environmental damage, and the vessel is in a safe condition and operational while the impact is assessed; Nakilat said it is coordinating with the vessel, its crew, and regional maritime security authorities.

Context

Qatar's Ministry of Foreign Affairs strongly condemned the attack as a dangerous act that threatens the safety of maritime navigation and the security of global energy supplies, said investigations are under way to identify those responsible, and said Qatar reserves the right to take all necessary measures. Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, the UAE, the Gulf Cooperation Council and others condemned the attack. More than 120 commercial ships have been hit in the region since the war began in February, according to International Maritime Organization and UKMTO data cited by Al Jazeera, and October was the worst month for shipping, with at least 29 ships hit.

Contested claims

No group has claimed the attack. Nakilat did not identify the objects that struck the vessel, did not name who was responsible, and did not give the exact location, so it is not established whether the ship was inside the Strait of Hormuz or in the wider Gulf.

Why this matters for the US

The Gulf carries a large share of the world's traded liquefied natural gas and oil, and it is a US Navy operating area. A strike on a Qatari LNG carrier — with no claim and no attribution — widens the shipping threat beyond oil tankers to gas exports, raises energy and insurance costs, and adds another unattributed attack that could be misread and pull US forces in.

Confidence

High for the Nakilat facts (vessel, date, no injuries, no environmental damage, vessel operational) — the statement was published through the Qatar Stock Exchange and corroborated by Qatar's Foreign Ministry; no independent confirmation of what struck the ship or who was responsible.
