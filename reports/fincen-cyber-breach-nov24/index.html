FinCEN Systems Breached

US financial crimes bureau discloses 'cybersecurity incident'

mediumVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Cyber / FinCEN breach · Tuesday, November 24, 2026 at 7:00 PM CT

In plain English

The Treasury Department's financial-crimes bureau, FinCEN, says hackers got into some of its computer systems, and it has taken some systems offline as a precaution. FinCEN holds the suspicious activity reports banks file about possible money laundering and terrorist financing. Officials have not said who did it, what was taken, or when it happened — and the bureau's filing website is still running.

Full brief

What happened

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), the Treasury Department bureau that collects banks' suspicious activity reports, disclosed on Tuesday, Nov 24 that it is investigating a cybersecurity incident that 'resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems,' according to an alert on its Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing site first reported by The Verge. FinCEN has taken some systems offline as a precaution; the BSA E-Filing website itself remains operational, though some services may be temporarily unavailable. The same day, FinCEN issued Order FIN-2026-R002, granting temporary relief because a 'service disruption' is hindering filings — Bank Secrecy Act reports and beneficial ownership reports due between Nov 18 and Dec 29, 2026 can now be filed by Dec 30, 2026. The relief reaches back to submissions due from Nov 18, six days before the alert; FinCEN has not said when the intrusion began. The Financial Institutions Hotline (1-866-556-3974) is not affected.

Context

FinCEN's job is to safeguard the financial system from illicit activity by collecting, analyzing and sharing financial intelligence, including suspicious activity reports and beneficial ownership information. The disclosure came hours after the New York Times reported a breach of the CIA and ODNI open-source intelligence network (already on this dashboard). Contested / unverified: who is responsible (no attribution has been made), whether any data was taken (FinCEN has not said), which systems were reached, and whether the incident is related to the CIA/ODNI breach (FinCEN has not linked it to any other incident, and The Verge says it is unclear whether they are related). Claims of a department-wide Treasury compromise are not supported — the alert refers only to a limited subset of FinCEN systems.

Why this matters for the US

FinCEN holds Americans' financial transaction data and the suspicious activity reports banks file on possible money laundering and terrorist financing — a breach there risks sensitive financial intelligence reaching the wrong hands — and it is the second federal breach disclosed the same day, showing US government systems remain exposed.

⚠ Attacker unidentified — no attribution has been made; FinCEN has not said whether any data was taken or which systems were reached; no confirmed link to the CIA/ODNI breach.
Confidence

High that FinCEN disclosed a breach and took systems offline — FinCEN's own alert on its BSA E-Filing site, reported by The Verge, corroborated by its same-day relief order FIN-2026-R002; the attacker, the data taken, and any link to the CIA/ODNI breach are unknown.
