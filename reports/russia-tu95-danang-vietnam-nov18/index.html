Russian Bombers in Vietnam

Russian Bombers Rattle American Allies Days Before Trump-Putin-Xi Summit (Daily Caller, relaying Reuters, Nov 18, 2026)

low-mediumVerifiedChina–Taiwan · Strategic bombers — Vietnam · Wednesday, November 18, 2026 at 2:52 PM CT

In plain English

Two Russian Tu-95 strategic bombers — nuclear-capable 'Bear' bombers — flew into Da Nang, Vietnam on Tuesday and were still there Wednesday, Reuters reported, the first such deployment since 2014. It comes a day after Russian aircraft violated Japanese airspace and entered Taiwan's air-defence zone, and days before the Trump-Putin-Xi summit in Shenzhen.

Full brief

What happened

Two Tu-95 strategic bombers, plus two support aircraft, arrived at Da Nang International Airport in Vietnam on Tuesday and were still there on Wednesday, Reuters reported. It is the first known Russian strategic-aviation deployment to Vietnam since 2014, when aerial-refuelling aircraft supported bomber missions near US installations on Guam. Reuters reported there was no indication nuclear weapons accompanied the aircraft.

Context

the deployment came a day after Russian aircraft violated Japanese airspace and entered Taiwan's air-defence zone — a separate incident, already on this dashboard — and days before the Trump-Putin-Xi summit in Shenzhen. Contested / unconfirmed — the bombers' presence is Reuters' reporting, not an official Russian or Vietnamese statement; neither government has confirmed the aircraft type or the purpose of the deployment.

Why this matters for the US

The deployment puts nuclear-capable Russian bombers back in Southeast Asia within reach of US Pacific hubs and rattles US treaty allies Japan, South Korea and Taiwan days before the summit.
