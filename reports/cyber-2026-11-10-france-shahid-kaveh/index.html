France blames Iran hackers

France attributes supermarket refrigeration cyberattack to IRGC unit Shahid Kaveh

mediumVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Cyber / IRGC attribution · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 at 6:00 AM CT

In plain English

France says an IRGC cyber unit hacked supermarket refrigeration systems across about 700 stores in October, spoiling food worth tens of millions of euros. French prosecutors want arrest warrants for the unit's leaders. Iran denies it.

Full brief

On the night of Oct 11–12, attackers took remote control of Danfoss/Dixell refrigeration controllers at about 700 French retail sites (Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, Intermarché, plus small butchers, abattoirs and food plants), pushed defrost settings to high temperatures (up to 50°C) and deactivated alarms — food losses in the tens of millions of euros and roughly 15–20 fires were reported. On Nov 9–10, France's cyber crisis coordination centre (ANSSI, DGSE, DGSI, DGA, COMCYBER) attributed the attack to Shahid Kaveh, an offensive cyber unit of the IRGC's Electronic and Cyber Centre. The Paris prosecutor opened a judicial investigation and sought international arrest warrants for the unit's head and two deputies. Iran's Foreign Ministry rejected the allegations. Boundaries: France did NOT attribute the Nov 2–3 refrigeration wave in Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Finland, Luxembourg, Netherlands and Sweden, nor the Nov 1–2 incident at French blood-service cold stores — those are not presented here as Iranian. US link: the same IRGC unit was previously linked to industrial-controller attacks on US water utilities, and US agencies (CISA, FBI, HHS, USDA, DOT) issued a joint advisory around Nov 10 on industrial refrigeration controllers and cold-chain monitoring.

⚠ French government attribution — not a court finding; Iran rejects the allegations
Confidence

High that France made this attribution (ANSSI-led cyber crisis coordination centre, Nov 9–10); the attribution is a government claim, not a court finding.
