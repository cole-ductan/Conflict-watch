Hodeidah Port Struck

Saudi coalition strikes Hodeidah port as Houthis target Riyadh and warn airlines off Saudi airspace (Reuters/AP, Nov 20, 2026)

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Houthi missiles — Saudi Arabia · Friday, November 20, 2026 at 9:22 AM CT

In plain English

The Saudi-led coalition bombed the Houthi-held port of Hodeidah in Yemen on Friday, saying it was destroying sites used to smuggle weapons and ballistic missiles. The Houthis fired back at Saudi Arabia — the coalition says it shot down five drones and a ballistic missile on Friday morning, with Riyadh among the targets, and two more ballistic missiles toward Jazan later in the day. The Houthis told airlines to stay out of Saudi airspace and told shipping companies to pull their ships out of Saudi Red Sea ports. The Houthis say they hit a power station and a port in southern Saudi Arabia, but that has not been independently verified, and the coalition reported no damage.

Full brief

What happened

The Saudi-led coalition said on Friday, Nov 20, that it had struck the Red Sea port of Hodeidah in Yemen to destroy Houthi capabilities, after warning civilians to evacuate the area, Reuters reported. The coalition said it destroyed operational capabilities at the port used for smuggling weapons and transporting ballistic missiles; Yemen's Information Minister said the port remains fully operational and the flow of goods will continue. The coalition also said it carried out an operation in Yemen's capital, Sanaa, early Friday targeting ballistic missile and drone capabilities, claiming seven targets destroyed including six workshops used to make and assemble ballistic missile launchers and drones plus a supply depot, with operations in Saada and Taiz ongoing. The Associated Press reported the coalition intercepted five drones and one ballistic missile fired by the Houthis on Friday morning, with Riyadh among the targets. The coalition later said it intercepted and destroyed two more ballistic missiles toward the southern province of Jazan, with shrapnel falling in the Shuqaiq area. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the group attacked a port in Jazan and a power station in the Al-Shuqaiq area, and warned airlines to avoid Saudi airspace because it will be the scene of our operations in the coming hours, except near the Muslim holy cities of Mecca and Medina. He also warned workers near Saudi Red Sea ports to evacuate and urged shipping companies to evacuate their ships from those ports, threatening Jeddah and other Red Sea ports if the coalition attacked Hodeidah.

Context

the exchange comes a day after the Houthis struck the tallest skyscraper in Riyadh, and is the fifth day of the renewed Saudi-Houthi exchange since the Nov 18 sequence already on this dashboard.

Contested claims

the Houthis' claims of hitting the Shuqaiq power station and the Jazan port are unverified; the coalition acknowledged debris fell in Shuqaiq but reported no damage. The coalition's claim of seven targets destroyed in Sanaa, and its account of what the Hodeidah port facilities were used for, are its own statements and have not been independently verified. A Houthi television claim of a coalition air raid on a power station in Mocha is likewise unverified.

Why this matters for the US

The Houthis are now threatening civilian air travel and Red Sea shipping out of the ports of a close US ally, a day after landing a strike on Riyadh's tallest tower — the fight is widening from probing Saudi defences to holding Saudi airspace, ports and the capital at risk, next to US personnel and facilities in the kingdom and on Red Sea trade routes.
