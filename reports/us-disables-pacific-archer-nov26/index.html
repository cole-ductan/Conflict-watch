US Disables Blockade Runner

US disables ship trying to leave Iran, as Tehran vows to confront blockade

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Blockade enforcement — Gulf of Oman · Friday, November 27, 2026 at 11:00 AM CT

In plain English

The US military disabled a cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday after it tried to run the American naval blockade of Iran, US Central Command said on Friday. A US aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged bulk carrier M/V Pacific Archer, knocking out its propulsion; the civilian crew was unharmed. The ship had just left the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas. It is the 15th vessel the US says it has disabled since the blockade began, and the sixth since the blockade resumed this summer.

Full brief

What happened

US Central Command said on Friday, November 27, 2026, that a US military aircraft launched precision munitions that struck the stern of the Palau-flagged bulk carrier M/V Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday, November 26, after the ship departed the Iranian port city of Bandar Abbas and tried to run the US naval blockade of Iranian ports despite multiple warnings. The ship's propulsion was disabled and the civilian crew was unharmed, CENTCOM said, releasing video of the strike.

Context

The US reimposed its naval blockade on Iranian ports in July, and earlier in November announced Task Force Iron Gate to restrict the movement of weapons and sanctioned materials across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea. CENTCOM says US forces have redirected 158 commercial vessels that complied with warnings and disabled six that refused to comply since the blockade resumed; the Pacific Archer is at least the 15th vessel disabled since the blockade began in the spring. UKMTO logged the incident as Advisory 224-26, recording an incident involving a cargo vessel and military forces in the Gulf of Oman on November 26. The ship's cargo and owner were not disclosed.

Contested claims

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Friday that two tankers tried to transit the Strait of Hormuz through an 'unauthorized' route, claiming one struck a naval mine and the other turned back; that claim has not been independently confirmed and is not treated as fact here. An adviser to Iran's supreme leader, Mohammad Mokhber, said Iran would keep the Strait of Hormuz shut while vowing to break the embargo, without giving details.

Why this matters for the US

The US is now using live fire against commercial ships that defy its blockade of Iran, in the same waters where Iranian forces are striking tankers and threatening US escorts — each disabling raises the odds of a miscalculation that hits a US warship or aircraft.

⚠ Blockade enforcement, not a new front — this is the latest in a series of US disabling strikes on blockade-runners, not the opening of a new campaign; no US personnel were involved in combat and no one was hurt.
Confidence

High that a US aircraft disabled the Pacific Archer on Thursday — US Central Command said so in a public statement and released video of the strike, and Reuters, the Associated Press and the Wall Street Journal all reported it. The ship's cargo and owner were not disclosed, and the exact time of the strike was not given.
