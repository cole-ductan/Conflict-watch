Engine Room Hit

UK maritime agency reports two tanker incidents near Oman; Iran says vessel hit mine east of Hormuz (Reuters, Nov 21, 2026)

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Maritime attack — Strait of Hormuz · Friday, November 20, 2026 at 11:50 PM CT

In plain English

Less than an hour after the first strike, a second tanker sailing out of the Strait of Hormuz was hit in its engine room by an unknown projectile, about 17 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman. No one was hurt, and the environmental impact is unknown. UKMTO has not named the ship or said who fired, and it is not clear whether the two Saturday warnings involve the same vessel or two different tankers.

Full brief

What happened

UKMTO Warning 216-26: a tanker on outbound transit through the Strait of Hormuz was struck in its engine room by an unknown projectile at 05:50 UTC on Saturday, Nov 21 (11:50 PM CT Friday, Nov 20), about 17 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman. No casualties were reported; the environmental impact is unconfirmed. UKMTO advised vessels to transit with caution while authorities investigate.

Context

the warning came 50 minutes after Warning 215-26, a stern strike on a tanker 11 NM north of Khasab. It is the fifth tanker struck near Khasab since Tuesday, after the Nov 17 ADNOC vessel NAVIG8 MESSI (13 NM north, one crew member missing, 11 injured), the Nov 18 engine-room strike (6 NM north, Warning 212-26, crew safe), and the Nov 20 port-bow strike with a fire on board (8 NM north, Warning 214-26, crew safe).

Contested claims

UKMTO says 'unknown projectile' and has not attributed the strike. Iran's navy said on Saturday that an oil tanker caught fire after hitting a naval mine east of the Strait of Hormuz; Reuters could not independently verify that account or establish whether it referred to either of the UKMTO incidents. Reuters reported it was not immediately clear whether the two Saturday warnings involved the same vessel.

Why this matters for the US

The Strait of Hormuz is the chokepoint for Gulf oil and a US Navy operating area. Two tankers hit within an hour — the fifth strike near Khasab in five days — keeps the pressure on US and allied shipping, pushes up energy and insurance costs, and raises the chance of a miscalculation that pulls US forces in. The pattern, not any single hit, is the threat.

⚠ Attacker unidentified — vessel unnamed by UKMTO; no claim of responsibility.
Confidence

High for the UKMTO facts (warning number 216-26, 17 NM east of Khasab, unknown projectile in the engine room, no casualties — UKMTO's own warning). No attribution — UKMTO has not said who fired and no group has claimed the attack. Whether the two Saturday warnings involve one vessel or two is unresolved.
