Peoria bomb threat

Bomb threat and suspicious package at Peoria ethanol plant force evacuation of more than 5,000 people; package rendered safe, evacuation lifted Nov 14, FBI and ATF investigating

lowVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Homeland / Bomb threat — Illinois · Friday, November 13, 2026 at 1:08 PM CT

In plain English

A bomb threat at an ethanol plant in Peoria, Illinois forced more than 5,000 people to evacuate on Friday while bomb teams dealt with a suspicious package. The evacuation was lifted Saturday, nobody was hurt, and the FBI is now investigating. The police chief has not confirmed the package was actually a bomb, and officials say there is no known link to terrorism.

Full brief

BioUrja Renewables' ethanol plant at 1 Edmund Street in Peoria, Illinois reported a bomb threat at about 1:08 p.m. CT on Friday, and a suspicious package was found at about 2 p.m. Police deemed it a “credible threat” at about 6:45 p.m. and set a one-mile blast radius; more than 5,000 residents and workers were evacuated across Peoria and East Peoria, and I-74 was shut down. Bomb technicians rendered the package safe at about 9:12 p.m. Friday. The evacuation order was lifted at about 1 p.m. CT on Saturday, November 14, after a full search found no additional devices; roads reopened, there were no injuries, and no arrests, suspects, or motive have been announced. The FBI's Springfield office is now leading the criminal investigation with ATF, and ATF says there is no known connection to terrorism. On what the package was: Police Chief Brad Dixon would not confirm it was a bomb or an explosive — he called it a “credible package/threat” and said determining its composition is part of the ongoing process. Mayor Rita Ali described it as a bomb, and CNN described it as an “explosive device”; the device type remains unconfirmed and no attribution has been made. This is reported as a homeland security incident with the evacuation resolved and the investigation ongoing — not as a confirmed bombing or a terror attack.

⚠ Device type unconfirmed — chief has not confirmed a bomb; no attribution, no known terrorism link
