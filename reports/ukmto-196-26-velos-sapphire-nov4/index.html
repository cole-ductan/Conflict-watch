Fatal Tanker Strike

UKMTO Warning 196-26: tanker struck by two projectiles off Qatar; one seafarer killed

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Maritime attack — Gulf off Qatar · Wednesday, November 4, 2026 at 5:20 PM CT

In plain English

A tanker was hit by two projectiles in the Gulf north of Qatar late Wednesday, Nov 4. The ship caught fire, and one crew member was killed — the first confirmed death in this run of tanker attacks. The ship's manager later identified it as the Marshall Islands-flagged Velos Sapphire, sailing empty toward Bahrain. No one has claimed the attack, and no attacker has been identified.

Full brief

What happened

UKMTO Warning 196-26: a tanker was struck by two unidentified projectiles at 23:20 UTC on Wednesday, November 4, about 50 nautical miles north of Madinat ash Shamal, Qatar. The strike caused an explosion and fire on board. Casualties — one seafarer killed, per the ship's manager Velos Tankers (Reuters, Nov 6). UKMTO's own warning listed crew status as unconfirmed at issue time. Vessel — UKMTO did not name the vessel. Reuters (Nov 6) identified it as the MT Velos Sapphire — Marshall Islands-flagged, Greek-managed (Athens-based Velos Tankers), a 2007-built 75,000-dwt LR1 product tanker, sailing unladen toward Bahrain. Taken to Khalifa Bin Salman Port, Bahrain, after the incident. The Velos Sapphire does not appear on Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA) list of non-compliant ships. Attacker — not identified. No claim of responsibility; no government has publicly attributed it.

Context

earliest of the November UKMTO-verified tanker strikes; the dashboard's tanker coverage previously began Nov 10 (Warning 201-26). This card, with 197-26, 199-26 and 200-26, fills the Nov 4–7 gap — four verified strikes in four days before the dashboard's coverage starts.

Why this matters for the US

a fatal strike on a tanker on the Gulf's main trade route, days into a campaign of projectile attacks on commercial shipping, directly threatens US shipping and trade and raises war-risk costs for American energy and commercial traffic.

⚠ First confirmed fatality of the tanker campaign — attacker unidentified, no claim of responsibility
