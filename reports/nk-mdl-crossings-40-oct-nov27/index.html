NK Troops Cross Border

N. Korean troops cross inter-Korean border about 40 times over past 2 months: lawmaker

mediumVerifiedNorth Korea · Border incursions — DMZ · November 27, 2026 · time not stated by the source

In plain English

North Korean soldiers have crossed the Military Demarcation Line (MDL) — the heavily guarded border with South Korea, inside the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) — about 40 times in the last two months — roughly 30 times in October and 10 in November — and they kept doing it even after a North Korean mine blast wounded three South Korean soldiers in September. South Korea answers with warning broadcasts and warning shots, but the crossings have not stopped.

Full brief

What happened

North Korean troops crossed the Military Demarcation Line about 40 times since October — about 30 incursions in October and about 10 in November — according to data on border fortification work and demarcation-line incursions that South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff submitted to Rep. Yu Yong-weon of the People Power Party, a member of the National Assembly's National Defense Committee, on Friday. The Joint Chiefs had earlier told Yu's office that North Korean troops crossed the line about 20 times in August and about 30 times in September — roughly 90 crossings in four months. The incursions continued even after the Sept. 21 explosion of North Korea-planted mines south of the Demilitarized Zone, which injured three South Korean soldiers; the U.N. Command deemed that blast an armistice violation on Sept. 30. North Korea is expanding border fortification work — laying mines and installing barriers, barbed wire and anti-tank obstacles. South Korea's military responds to approaches with warning broadcasts and to actual crossings with warning shots from guard posts. Rep. Yu called on the defense ministry and the South Korean military to take a practical, firm response, saying the current deterrence is not working.

Contested claims

The crossing counts are South Korean military detections, mostly occurring during North Korean border construction work; Pyongyang has denied crossings in the past, and Kim Yo Jong has threatened 'merciless retaliation' if the South fires on her troops — that threat is the North Korean position, not established fact.

Why this matters for the US

South Korea is a U.S. treaty ally hosting about 28,500 American troops, so repeated armed crossings of the world's most heavily fortified border — after a mine blast that already wounded allied soldiers — raise the odds of an accidental clash that pulls U.S. forces in.

⚠ Crossings, not a clash — no new firefight and no casualties in the crossings themselves; the counts are South Korean military detections, and Pyongyang has denied crossings in the past.
Confidence

High on the counts — South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff data, submitted to the National Assembly and released by Rep. Yu Yong-weon of the People Power Party; reported consistently by Yonhap, Korea JoongAng Daily and Aju Press.
