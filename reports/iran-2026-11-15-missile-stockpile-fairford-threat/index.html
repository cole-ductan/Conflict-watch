Iran's missile threat endures

US intelligence puts Iran's missile stockpile at 1,500–2,500; state TV warns Khorramshahr can reach RAF Fairford

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Missile arsenal — Iran · Sunday, November 15, 2026 at 1:12 PM CT

In plain English

US intelligence now estimates that Iran still has between 1,500 and 2,500 ballistic missiles — roughly half of what it had before the war — even after months of American and Israeli strikes on its missile factories. Iran is rebuilding the arsenal from parts it stockpiled underground, and Israeli officials agree the number is above 1,500. On Sunday, Iranian state TV warned that a modified Khorramshahr missile could reach the US-UK air base at RAF Fairford in England, about 2,800 miles away. It is not clear the missile can actually fly that far.

Full brief

The US intelligence community assesses that Iran has between 1,500 and 2,500 missiles in its arsenal, US officials told The Wall Street Journal, leaving Tehran with significant capacity to attack US and allied targets if all-out war resumes. The officials described it as a low-confidence assessment, because it is hard to track how many missiles Iran has ready to launch or in storage — but they said it was clear Iran retained a formidable stockpile. Although the US and Israel damaged Iran's missile-production facilities, Iran has continued to rebuild its arsenal, albeit at a slower pace than before the war, mostly from stockpiled components, and it also has many drones and short-range missiles at its disposal. Israeli military officials similarly assess that Iran has more than 1,500 ballistic missiles. The assessment cuts against statements from administration officials, including President Trump, that Iran's military had been decimated, and it has informed internal Trump administration discussions about whether to relaunch strikes alongside Israel. Iran has also improved the effectiveness of its attacks on US military installations, radars and early-warning systems across the Middle East — including with help from allies such as Russia — and as a result many US military assets have been moved to Israel, which is considered safer. US allies in the Gulf have told the Trump administration they do not want a return to war, because Iran could target them with missiles and drones in retaliation. Separately on Sunday, Iranian state broadcasters warned that Iran's modified Khorramshahr ballistic missile could reach American military bases as far as Europe, specifically the US-UK RAF Fairford base about 2,800 miles away. The capability is unconfirmed: it remains unclear whether the missile can actually reach that far. The warning follows the IRGC's Nov 7 lifting of Iran's self-imposed 2,000-km ballistic-missile range limit, already covered on this dashboard.

Why this matters for the US

even after months of strikes, Iran retains enough missiles to threaten US forces and allied targets across the region — and is now publicly putting a US-operated base in Europe in its sights — which is why the assessment is driving debate inside the administration over whether to resume strikes.

⚠ Stockpile figure is a low-confidence US intelligence estimate, not a counted inventory; the Fairford range claim is Iranian state TV's claim, not a demonstrated capability
