Drones Enter NATO Skies

Drone briefly violates Latvian airspace, Latvian armed forces say

mediumVerifiedRussia–Ukraine · NATO airspace — Latvia & Estonia · Thursday, November 26, 2026 at 6:30 PM CT

In plain English

An unidentified drone briefly crossed into Latvia's airspace early on Friday, and NATO fighter jets were scrambled in response, Latvia's armed forces said. Hours later Estonia sounded an air-threat warning across six south-eastern counties and also scrambled NATO jets; Estonia's foreign minister said drones flying along the border had entered Estonian airspace too. No damage or casualties were reported, and both alerts were lifted within about an hour. Neither military identified the drone or said where it came from.

Full brief

What happened

An unidentified drone briefly violated Latvian airspace in the early hours of Friday, November 27, Latvia's National Armed Forces said in a statement carried by Reuters. The drone was detected near the country's eastern border, and an airspace threat alert was issued for the eastern Ludza municipality at 2:30 a.m. local time; NATO Baltic Air Policing fighter jets were scrambled and Latvia launched an interceptor drone, which later returned to its launch site. The Ludza alert was lifted after about 25 minutes and a lower-threat alert for the neighbouring Balvi municipality was also reversed. Hours later, at 6:41 a.m. local time, Estonia's Defence Forces issued an air-threat warning to six south-eastern counties — Tartu, Jõgeva, Viljandi, Valga, Võru and Põlva — and NATO Baltic Air Policing fighter jets were scrambled from Ämari Air Base. The warning was lifted at about 7:22 a.m. after the threat passed. Estonia's foreign minister, Margus Tsahkna, said drones flying parallel to Estonia's eastern border had also entered Estonian airspace, prompting the air-raid alert, and that the threat had passed.

Context

The alerts were issued as Russia's Leningrad region was repelling a drone attack in which the region's governor said more than 40 pilotless aircraft were downed. It is the second such alert in Estonia in a week, after a similar warning on November 21, and it follows the November 20 incursion in which Russian missiles and a drone entered Polish airspace — already on this dashboard — and a drone that crossed Lithuania before flying into Latvia weeks earlier.

Contested claims

Neither Latvia nor Estonia identified the drone or said where it came from, and neither said it was shot down. Some outlets have described the drone as Russian, but that attribution is not in the official statements. No authority has linked the Baltic alerts to the Ukrainian drone attack on Russia's Leningrad region; the timing coincides, but the connection is unconfirmed.

Why this matters for the US

Latvia and Estonia are NATO allies, and drones keep entering allied airspace during Russia's war on Ukraine, forcing NATO jets to scramble. Each incident tests the alliance's air defences and raises the risk of a miscalculation that could pull the United States in under its treaty commitments.
