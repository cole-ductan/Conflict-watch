Gulf Airspace Warning Extended

EASA extends Gulf conflict-zone airspace bulletin to January 31, 2027

lowVerifiedIran / Gulf · Aviation safety — Gulf airspace · November 16, 2026 · time not stated by the source

In plain English

Europe’s aviation safety agency has extended its warning for flights over the Gulf until the end of January, citing the continued volatility between the US and Iran. The warning covers the airspace over Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, the UAE and Oman. It is a routine renewal — the advice itself has not changed — and it signals that regulators still see the Gulf as a conflict zone.

Full brief

What happened

EASA extended its Conflict Zone Information Bulletin for the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman (CZIB-2026-07, revision R4) until January 31, 2027. The bulletin was originally issued July 14, 2026; its prior validity ran to November 16, 2026. Coverage — airspace over Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, the UAE and Oman (Bahrain, Kuwait, Doha, Emirates and Muscat FIRs). Change — none to the recommendations: a date extension only, citing continued volatility between the US and Iran. EASA reviews each bulletin at expiry or sooner if the situation changes.

Context

the extension follows the same pattern as EASA’s other Middle East bulletins (renewed without content change); the Gulf bulletin is one of six EASA Middle East bulletins that expired Nov 16. The FAA’s own Gulf guidance and the dashboard’s FAA ground-stop items cover the US side of the same airspace risk.

Why this matters for the US

US carriers, travelers and military flights transit Gulf airspace daily over states hosting US bases; a European regulator keeping the whole Gulf a conflict zone through January signals the assessed threat to civil aviation — and to the airspace US forces operate in — is not easing.

⚠ Routine renewal — recommendations unchanged; a date extension only
Confidence

High — Aviation Week’s reporting of EASA’s bulletin extension (CZIB-2026-07, revision R4), corroborated by Travel and Tour World; prior validity per Fly Safe’s Q3 2026 State of the Airspace report.
