Russian Agent Indicted

DOJ indicts Russian national for acting as an unregistered foreign agent targeting a Silicon Valley technology and defense company

mediumVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Cyber / Espionage · Wednesday, November 18, 2026 at 12:00 PM CT

In plain English

A Russian man living in California has been charged with secretly working for the Russian government. Prosecutors say he was told to watch the employees of a US company that builds technology for the military — following one woman in her car for nearly 30 miles, taking photos of workers' laptop screens in a coffee shop, and leaving his own laptop out so his handlers could try to grab information from nearby computers. He was arrested on October 30 and is still in jail.

Full brief

What happened

A federal grand jury in San Francisco indicted Igor D'iu, a 25-year-old Russian national, on charges of conspiring to act as an agent of a foreign government, acting as an agent of a foreign government without telling the US Attorney General, interstate stalking, and fraud involving identification documents, the Justice Department announced Wednesday, Nov 18. D'iu was arrested Oct 30 and remains in federal custody; he first appeared in court Nov 2 and is next due Nov 24. According to the indictment and FBI affidavit, D'iu entered the United States unlawfully in the summer of 2026 after being recruited through Telegram by co-conspirators who told him his work was for "the Motherland" and directed him to gather information about a large technology and defense company with an office in Palo Alto, California, because it supplies weapons used against Russia in Ukraine. The affidavit says he was told to visit a coffee shop near the company's office, identify employees, and photograph their laptop screens while they worked, and to position his laptop at designated spots so his handlers could remotely access it and try to collect information from company employees nearby. On Sept 3, he allegedly followed a female employee for nearly 30 miles from Palo Alto toward San Francisco, running a red light and making illegal turns as she tried to evade him, until she drove to a San Francisco police station; investigators say he followed other employees on preceding days. FBI agents searching D'iu and his East Palo Alto home in October recovered about $18,000 in cash, two laptops, two iPhones, two Russian passports, wireless networking equipment, and fraudulent ID documents, plus a notebook with 10 handwritten addresses, many matching company employees' homes, and detailed profiles of at least three employees.

Contested claims

The Justice Department did not name the company; news reports identify it as Palantir, which has not been confirmed officially. Press coverage describes the Telegram handlers as suspected Russian government representatives, possibly the FSB; the Justice Department release itself calls them co-conspirators and does not name an agency. An indictment is an allegation only; D'iu is presumed innocent until proven guilty.

Why this matters for the US

A foreign government allegedly ran an operative on American soil to stalk and surveil the employees of a US defense-technology company — a direct threat to US personnel and to the companies that build America's defense technology.
