Kim Expands Tactical Nukes

North Korea's Kim visits nuclear facilities, calls for wider tactical nuclear weapons deployment (Reuters, Nov 25, 2026)

mediumVerifiedNorth Korea · Nuclear weapons — tactical deployment · Wednesday, November 25, 2026 at 3:30 PM CT

In plain English

North Korea's leader Kim Jong Un visited the country's nuclear weapons facilities and said North Korea should put tactical nuclear weapons — smaller nuclear weapons meant for battlefield use — into service across more parts of its military. He said North Korea is steadily making more nuclear material and that its status as a nuclear-armed country can never be taken away. This is a statement and a visit, not a missile launch or an attack.

Full brief

What happened

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un inspected nuclear material production and weapons research facilities and said the country should 'focus on utilizing tactical nuclear weapons across multiple domains for different missions,' state media KCNA reported on Thursday (Nov 26 in Pyongyang; Nov 25 in the US), according to Reuters. Kim said North Korea's nuclear material production capacity 'was being systematically expanded' and called recent achievements in nuclear weapons production a major success in carrying out the country's nuclear force policy. He declared North Korea's status as a nuclear weapons state 'absolutely inviolable' despite calls for denuclearisation, and KCNA quoted him saying 'Our nuclear capability is now a reality.' KCNA did not identify the facilities or their locations.

Context

the visit comes a week after the IAEA said North Korea is expanding its uranium-enrichment plant at Yongbyon (see that report), and after North Korea's Nov 4 short-range missile tests; President Trump has said he remains open to meeting Kim. No launch or attack was reported with this visit.

Why this matters for the US

Tactical nuclear weapons are designed for battlefield use against US and allied forces in South Korea and Japan, where about 28,500 and 54,000 US troops are stationed — a declared push to deploy them more widely raises the stakes for those forces and for US treaty allies.

⚠ Statement and capability development — no launch or attack
Confidence

High that KCNA published the statement and the visit as reported by Reuters and CNN; KCNA did not name the facilities or their locations, so which sites Kim visited is unconfirmed. No launch or attack occurred with this visit.
