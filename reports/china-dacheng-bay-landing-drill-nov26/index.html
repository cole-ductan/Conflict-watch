China Rehearses Taiwan Landing

China tests new Taiwan landing tactics in coastal drills

mediumVerifiedChina–Taiwan · Amphibious drills — Taiwan Strait · Thursday, November 26, 2026 at 10:34 PM CT

In plain English

China has been running a weeks-long practice landing drill on its coast across from Taiwan, satellite photos show. Instead of purpose-built military landing craft, it linked up civilian ships — barges and semi-submersible vessels — into a floating bridge about half a mile long to move heavy equipment ashore. The drill is still going on, and Taiwan's defence ministry says it is aware of it and weighing what it means. This is practice, not an attack — but it is practice for landing troops on an island.

Full brief

What happened

China is staging a weeks-long amphibious landing drill in Dacheng Bay, in Fujian province across the strait from Taiwan, testing a new method of landing troops ashore by linking civilian vessels into a makeshift floating bridge, Reuters reported on Thursday, citing satellite images and a report by geospatial data company ingeniSPACE that Reuters reviewed. The exercise was first spotted on November 1. Analysis of images from November 1 to 16 showed the vessels manoeuvring around the bay, forming and dismantling a floating bridge about 800 metres (2,600 ft) long. Eight civilian vessels took part — four barges, two semi-submersible and two powered — along with four landing craft, smaller support vessels and Chinese navy ships nearby. No purpose-built bridging equipment was observed. A senior security official briefed on the drill confirmed it was still in progress on Friday and said it was designed to 'practise the swift seizure of small islands' and was 'very targeted' at contingencies along the first island chain, the arc of islands from Japan through Taiwan and the Philippines.

Context

The official said the drill appeared to be part of this year's 'All-Military Strategic Exercise' and was routine, but held later than in previous years. Taiwan has previously disclosed Chinese amphibious landing drills in Dacheng Bay in 2023 and 2024 involving roll-on/roll-off freighters, both in September. Reuters documented similar drills off Guangdong in August last year, showing China rehearsing the use of civilian shipping to supplement a Taiwan operation. Jamming activity was observed in the area of the drill. Taiwan's defence ministry said it was aware of Chinese military activity in Dacheng Bay, including the use of civilian vessels in exercises, and was weighing the implications for security in the Taiwan Strait and peace in the region.

Contested claims

ingeniSPACE's chief operating officer said the PLA 'appeared to be rehearsing low-signature methods to provide logistical support to forces that could be involved in a first island chain invasion scenario'; that is the company's assessment, not a confirmed Chinese plan. The senior official cautioned against overstating the drill's battlefield value, saying 'there is still some distance between innovation and fantasy' and that the barges would be 'live targets' in wartime. China's defence ministry did not respond to a request for comment; it said on Thursday the training was 'entirely legitimate and reasonable.'

Why this matters for the US

Taiwan is the central flashpoint for US forces in the Pacific, and China is now openly rehearsing how to get heavy equipment ashore on an island using ordinary civilian ships — a method that would let it land troops at places beyond the beaches its landing craft can reach, complicating US and allied planning for a Taiwan contingency.

⚠ Drill, not an attack — a rehearsal of landing tactics, described by a senior official as part of the routine annual exercise, held later than usual. The invasion-scenario framing is an analyst assessment, not a confirmed Chinese plan.
Confidence

High that the drill is happening and that civilian vessels formed a floating bridge in Dacheng Bay — satellite images from multiple commercial providers (ingeniSPACE, Vantor, Planet Labs) reviewed by Reuters, and a senior security official confirmed the report's details and that the drill is still in progress. The 'invasion scenario' framing is the geospatial company's assessment, and the official cautioned against overstating the drill's battlefield value.
