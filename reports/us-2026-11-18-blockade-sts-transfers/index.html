Blockade Widens to Ship Transfers

US tightens Iran blockade with curbs on oil transfers at sea, advisory shows (Reuters, Nov 18, 2026)

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Blockade expansion — ship-to-ship transfers · Wednesday, November 18, 2026 at 10:40 AM CT

In plain English

The US military said on Wednesday, Nov 18, that any ship taking cargo at sea from a vessel loaded in Iran will be treated as trying to dodge the US blockade of Iranian ports. Those ships can be stopped, boarded, diverted, captured, or sent back to the Iranian port the cargo came from — and ships that ignore orders could face enforcement action, including the use of force. The blockade itself has been in place since July 14, after a brief lifting in June.

Full brief

What happened

US Central Command (CENTCOM) issued an updated advisory on Wednesday, Nov 18, expanding its blockade of Iranian ports: any ship that receives cargo through a ship-to-ship transfer from a vessel carrying cargo loaded at an Iranian port or coastline may be considered to be evading the blockade, even if it never entered Iranian ports or waters, Reuters reported. CENTCOM said such ships 'may be stopped, boarded, diverted, captured, or directed to proceed to the Iranian port where the cargo originated,' and ships that fail to comply immediately with lawful orders could face enforcement action, 'including the use of force consistent with applicable law.' The advisory also says the blockade will not impede neutral transit through the Strait of Hormuz to or from non-Iranian destinations, though neutral vessels may still be subject to visit and search.

Context

the US naval blockade on Iran-related oil exports was first imposed April 13, lifted in June after Washington reached a draft understanding with Tehran, and reimposed July 14 after the agreement collapsed. Reuters reported in October that Iran has still managed to export some liquefied petroleum gas and jet fuel since July, while its crude exports have remained near a standstill for months.

Why this matters for the US

The US is closing the at-sea loophole Iran has used to keep exporting fuel despite the blockade — and putting teeth behind it, with boarding, capture, and use of force now explicitly on the table for ships that take Iranian cargo at sea. That raises the stakes for any tanker in the Gulf, and for US forces doing the enforcing.

Confidence

High that CENTCOM issued the updated advisory on Nov 18 (Reuters reporting of the CENTCOM/NAVCENT advisory text, corroborated by the NAVCENT update posted the same day). High that Iran has continued exporting some LPG and jet fuel since July despite the blockade (Reuters, citing shipping intelligence and security sources in October).
