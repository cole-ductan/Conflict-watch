Cyberattacks on Energy Tankers

US eyes Iran as possible culprit in dozens of new cyberattacks against ships, WSJ reports

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Cyber / Espionage · Monday, November 23, 2026 at 6:34 PM CT

In plain English

The US is investigating whether Iran is behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 energy tankers carrying oil, liquefied petroleum gas, and chemicals, the Wall Street Journal reported on Monday. Some ships got warnings and bomb threats saying they could be blown up, and the US Coast Guard has sent cyber teams to at least four ships as they approached American ports. Iran is the main suspect, but US officials also say Russia could be behind the attacks — and one Trump administration official said the US has found no evidence proving Iran did it.

Full brief

What happened

The United States is investigating Iran's potential involvement in dozens of cyberattacks against energy tankers, the Wall Street Journal reported on Monday, November 23, 2026 (Reuters, Nov 23). As many as 50 ships carrying oil, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), chemicals, and other hazardous cargo are believed to have been attacked, the Journal said, citing US officials and others familiar with the matter. Some ships received warnings and bomb threats saying the vessels could be blown up; others were told not to load American products, and some received ransom demands. The US Coast Guard has deployed cyber protection teams to at least four of the ships as they approached the United States in recent weeks. The attackers exploited a vulnerability in Fortinet routers widely used in the maritime industry.

Context

The Journal reported in September on two of the attacks, involving the VL Prosperity and the Kohaku, which were heading to Texas when targeted. Another ship that received a bomb threat, the Vivit Africa, was carrying liquefied natural gas (LNG) from the US to Italy. In September, after the first known attacks, the US Coast Guard Cyber Command and the FBI issued an alert about nation-state actors exploiting Fortinet FortiGate devices used by shipping companies and maritime facilities. Early reports of some attacks have surfaced on Iranian and Russian websites and Telegram channels.

Why this matters for the US

the attacks target the energy shipping the US depends on and the American ports the ships are sailing toward — one threatened ship was carrying US liquefied natural gas to Italy, and the Coast Guard has had to board and clear ships before they could enter US ports. If a tanker carrying explosive cargo were actually blown up, the damage could close a port or a shipping lane.

⚠ Attribution is contested: Iran is the primary suspect according to US officials cited by the Journal, but a Trump administration official said the US has found no evidence indicating Iranian involvement, and Reuters could not immediately verify the report. Russia is also seen as a possible culprit. The four attacks the official described amounted to little more than a minor nuisance with no real impact.
Confidence
