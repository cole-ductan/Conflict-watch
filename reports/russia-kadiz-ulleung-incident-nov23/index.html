Russian Planes Near Korea

South Korea says several Russian aircraft flew into its air defense zone

mediumVerifiedNorth Korea · Korea Peninsula — air defense zone · Monday, November 23, 2026 at 4:52 PM CT

In plain English

Several Russian military aircraft flew into South Korea's air defence identification zone off its east coast on Monday, and came close enough to Ulleung Island that the government briefly told residents to take shelter. South Korea scrambled fighter jets, and the aircraft left during the military response. There was no violation of South Korean airspace, and no damage or injuries were reported. Russian aircraft have entered the zone several times this year, including a similar entry one week earlier — what was unusual this time was the shelter warning.

Full brief

What happened

Several Russian military aircraft entered the Korea Air Defense Identification Zone (KADIZ) over the East Sea on Tuesday morning, November 24, 2026 (Monday afternoon, November 23, US Central Time), and approached Ulleung Island, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said (AP, Nov 23; Reuters, Nov 23). South Korea's military had been tracking the aircraft, scrambled Air Force fighter jets, and coordinated a preemptive civil-defense warning: the Ministry of the Interior and Safety told Ulleung Island residents to take shelter in underground facilities or buildings at about 7:52 a.m. local time, and lifted the order about three minutes later, once the aircraft had left the danger area. The Joint Chiefs said there was no violation of South Korean sovereign airspace, and the aircraft departed eastward during the military response. The military did not say how many aircraft it detected or what types they were.

Context

An air defence identification zone is a buffer beyond a country's sovereign airspace; entering it is not itself a violation of sovereignty. Russia does not recognise South Korea's zone, and Russian aircraft have entered it several times this year, including a similar entry one week earlier, on November 17, that also prompted a fighter scramble. Early government messages caused confusion: the Interior Ministry first said a Russian aircraft had violated South Korean airspace, and a provincial message described the aircraft as suspected North Korean; the Joint Chiefs later corrected both points — the aircraft were Russian military planes, and no airspace violation occurred. South Korea's Defense Ministry said it would summon Russia's defense attaché to lodge a protest.

Why this matters for the US

South Korea is a US treaty ally hosting American troops, and Russian aircraft probing its air defences — this time close enough to trigger a rare civilian shelter alert — test the early-warning tripwire the United States is committed to help defend.

⚠ No violation of South Korean sovereign airspace occurred, and no damage or injuries were reported; the aircraft departed during South Korea's response.
Confidence

High on the ADIZ entry, the fighter scramble, and the shelter warning — South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff statement, carried by the Associated Press and Reuters, with the warning and its lifting on the public record. High that no sovereign airspace was violated — the Joint Chiefs stated it explicitly after correcting the earlier government messages.
