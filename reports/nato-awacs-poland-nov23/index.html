NATO AWACS Forward Deployed

NATO to send early-warning system aircraft to Poland after missiles incident

mediumVerifiedRussia–Ukraine · NATO airspace — Poland · Monday, November 23, 2026 at 11:51 AM CT

In plain English

NATO will send one of its AWACS early-warning radar planes to watch over its eastern flank after three Russian cruise missiles landed in Poland on Friday, the alliance said on Monday. Poland's deputy defence minister said the plane 'will be made available to Poland' after an emergency NATO meeting in Brussels. NATO's top military commander authorised the deployment and is looking at sending more capabilities. Where exactly the plane will be based is not settled in public — one report says Lithuania, not Poland.

Full brief

What happened

NATO will deploy an AWACS early-warning system aircraft to Poland as part of its response to three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles landing in the country on Friday, Polish Deputy Defence Minister Pawel Zalewski said on Monday, November 23, 2026, after a meeting of the North Atlantic Council in Brussels (Reuters, Nov 23). NATO spokeswoman Allison Hart confirmed that the Supreme Allied Commander Europe, US Air Force Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, authorised the forward deployment of a NATO AWACS aircraft, and said SACEUR is also examining the relocation of further military capabilities.

Context

Polish prosecutors confirmed on Sunday that debris from all three objects that entered Poland on Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, manufactured in the fourth quarter of 2026 at a plant in Dubna, Russia. The incursion happened during a massive Russian missile-and-drone attack on Ukraine; no one was injured in Poland. Poland called the emergency NATO session, and the alliance called the violations a dangerous incident for which Russia bears full responsibility. NATO's 14 AWACS aircraft, based at Geilenkirchen in Germany, have flown surveillance along the alliance's eastern flank since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Why this matters for the US

Poland is a NATO treaty ally hosting US forces, and the US general who commands NATO's military operations authorised this deployment. Russian missiles landing on allied soil is the kind of spillover that can pull the alliance in, and NATO is now hardening the airspace the US is committed to defend.

⚠ Basing is contested: the Polish deputy minister said the AWACS will be made available to Poland; dpa reported from SHAPE that the aircraft is headed to Lithuania. The deployment decision itself is confirmed by NATO.
Confidence

High on the deployment decision — Reuters wire, confirmed by both the Polish deputy defence minister and a NATO spokeswoman, with SACEUR's authorisation on the record. Medium on where the aircraft will be based — Zalewski said it will be made available to Poland, while dpa, citing NATO military headquarters in Mons, reported the approval concerns relocation to Lithuania.
