Pentagon Rethinks Bahrain Base

After heavy strikes by Iran, U.S. naval base in Bahrain faces uncertain future

mediumVerifiedIran / Gulf · Fifth Fleet posture — Bahrain · Friday, November 27, 2026 at 9:15 AM CT

In plain English

The Pentagon is now openly weighing whether the Navy's main Gulf base in Bahrain — Naval Support Activity Bahrain, home of the Fifth Fleet — will ever go back to how it was before Iranian strikes wrecked it. The Navy only recently started formally studying its future there and has no plan yet. The Congressional Research Service estimates the damage at more than $400 million, and only a small fraction of the 8,000-plus people who lived and worked there remain.

Full brief

What happened

Naval Support Activity Bahrain served as headquarters for U.S. Naval Forces Central Command and the U.S. 5th Fleet; Bahrain has hosted a U.S. naval command headquarters since 1948, and the U.S. assumed control of the installation after Britain's withdrawal in 1971. Iranian missile and drone attacks since Feb. 28 damaged the base; preliminary estimates cited by the Congressional Research Service put damage at $400 million or more, with destruction still visible from nearby highways. Only a fraction of the more than 8,000 military personnel, dependents, and civilian contractors once based there remain, leaving previously thriving neighborhoods largely deserted. Two Navy officials told the Post on condition of anonymity that the service began evaluating its future presence in Bahrain only recently and has yet to develop specific plans. 'Until all of this is resolved, we're not going anywhere near there,' one official said, referring to renewed Iranian attacks on Bahrain. 'It just doesn't make any sense.' The Navy could direct its Middle East operations from the United States without fully rebuilding the installation and maintain relationships with Gulf allies through periodic visits. In a statement to the Post, the Navy said it had 'nothing to announce at this time,' adding that it 'cannot speak to internal deliberations.' Bahrain's government declined to address specific questions about the installation's future but stressed a strategic partnership spanning eight decades.

Context

Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told The Epoch Times in September that Iran 'blew the hell out of Bahrain'; Adm. Daryl Caudle, chief of naval operations, said during an August town hall that the Navy is 'not getting back in there anytime soon'; on March 2 the State Department ordered nonemergency U.S. government personnel and family members to evacuate Bahrain; the Post reported in August that the Pentagon was weighing a reduction in America's Gulf footprint, including possibly shifting forces to Israel and Jordan. The Post reported two civilians were killed and dozens wounded in Iranian attacks on Bahrain, among the wounded two War Department employees.

Contested claims

The $400 million-plus damage figure is a Congressional Research Service preliminary estimate, and the evaluation's status rests on two anonymous Navy officials; no decision has been made, and shifting forces to Israel or Jordan is only a possibility the Pentagon weighed in August, not a decided relocation.

Why this matters for the US

The headquarters of the Fifth Fleet — the Navy's main hub for the Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz — may never reopen as it was, which would force the U.S. to run its Gulf naval operations from farther away or from other countries, weakening its ability to protect shipping and respond to Iran.

⚠ Posture deliberation, not a decision — no new attack, and no decision has been made about the base's future; relocation to Israel or Jordan was only weighed as a possibility in August.
