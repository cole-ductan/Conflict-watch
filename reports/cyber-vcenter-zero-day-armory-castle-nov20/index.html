vCenter Zero-Day Exploited

China-linked hackers exploited critical VMware vCenter zero-day in attacks since September (Mandiant/Broadcom, Nov 20, 2026)

mediumVerifiedCyber / Homeland · Cyber / Espionage · Thursday, November 19, 2026 at 6:26 PM CT

In plain English

Security researchers say a China-linked hacking group has been exploiting a serious flaw in VMware vCenter — the software many companies use to run their virtual servers — since early September. The flaw lets an attacker log in as an administrator without a password. Broadcom released a fix on November 19. The group used it to take over a vCenter server inside a network it had already broken into, and to steal data.

Full brief

What happened

Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group reported on Nov 20 that the suspected China-nexus espionage group Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221) exploited CVE-2026-123890, a CVSS 9.8 authentication bypass in VMware vCenter Server's Directory Service, in early September 2026. The flaw leaks a Secure Remote Password session key through the directory service's RPC interface, letting a remote unauthenticated attacker open a session as the built-in vCenter administrator. The attackers used a custom Go utility, BONESAW, to exfiltrate the directory database, hijack the SAML token service and create a new single-sign-on administrator account, and deployed web shells and backdoors (SLIMSAVAGE, CAGEMATCH, SAWSNAKE, LAZYBONES, BEARBONES). The vCenter exploitation was one stage of a longer intrusion that began with a BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day (CVE-2026-102764, exploited since late August and added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Oct 16). Broadcom patched CVE-2026-123890 on Nov 19 in VMSA-2026-0009 (vCenter 8.0 U3m, 9.0.2.0200, 9.1.1.0200) but did not confirm in-the-wild exploitation in its own advisory. This is a disclosure of September activity, not a new incident, and no victim was named.

Contested claims

Mandiant's BONESAW binary carries a July 13, 2026 build timestamp, suggesting the group may have known of the flaw before Broadcom's July 29 fix for the related CVE-2026-59309, but build metadata can be modified, so that date is not conclusive.

Why this matters for the US

vCenter is the control plane for virtualized infrastructure across US enterprises and government; a China-nexus group holding a near-maximum-severity administrator bypass for two months means unpatched US systems were exposed to full takeover and data theft, and the same group chains zero-days across enterprise appliances.

⚠ Retrospective disclosure of September 2026 exploitation — not a new incident; no victim named
Confidence

High that Broadcom patched CVE-2026-123890 on Nov 19 (VMSA-2026-0009) and that Mandiant attributes exploitation since early September to Armory Castle (Mandiant/Google Threat Intelligence Group report, Nov 20; BleepingComputer, Nov 19). Medium on the exact victim count and scope — no victim was named. The July 13 BONESAW build timestamp is not conclusive evidence the group knew of the flaw before July 29. Broadcom has not confirmed exploitation in its own advisory.
