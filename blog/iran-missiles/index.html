Iran's Missiles: What They Can Reach and What Can Stop Them

Iran has one of the largest missile arsenals in the Middle East — ballistic missiles that can reach Israel and US bases across the Gulf, and a newer hypersonic missile it says cannot be stopped. This post explains what Iran actually has, what is happening with those missiles right now, and answers the questions people are actually asking about them.

The direct answer: Iran can reach Israel and US forces across the region with missiles it already has, its most advanced missile is the hypersonic Fattah, and there is no public, verified record of a Fattah being intercepted — or of the US shooting down a hypersonic missile in combat.

That does not mean Iran's missiles are unstoppable, or that every claim about them is true. It means the honest picture is split: the range and the launches are verified, while the hardest questions — interception, exact stockpile numbers, and stealth detection — have no definitive public answer. Each one is answered plainly below, with links to the Conflict Ready USA dashboard.

Iran's Missile Arsenal — Why It Matters

Iran built its missile force as its main way to strike back. Its air force flies older aircraft, so missiles — ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and drones — are how Iran can hit targets hundreds or thousands of kilometers away without putting pilots in the air.

The arsenal matters for three reasons. First, range: Iran's medium-range ballistic missiles can reach Israel, US bases in Bahrain, Qatar, Iraq, and Saudi Arabia, and shipping across the Gulf. Second, volume: Iran is believed to have thousands of ballistic missiles and the ability to produce more, so it can fire in salvos designed to overwhelm defenses. Third, the new generation: the Fattah hypersonic missile is designed to fly faster and maneuver in flight, which is specifically meant to make interception harder.

None of that means every Iranian claim about its missiles is verified. Iran routinely describes its systems in the strongest possible terms. The dashboard separates what is verified — launches, strikes, and interceptions confirmed by named sources — from what is claimed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Iran's most powerful missile?

Iran's most advanced missile is the Fattah, a hypersonic missile Iran unveiled in 2023. Iran claims the Fattah-1 can reach speeds of Mach 13–15 and maneuver to evade missile defenses. The Fattah-2, unveiled later, is also described as hypersonic. Iran also has the Khorramshahr, a longer-range ballistic missile (around 2,000 km) that can carry a large warhead. Which is "most powerful" depends on what you mean — the Fattah is the most advanced (hypersonic, hardest to intercept), while the Khorramshahr has the longest range and heaviest payload. The dashboard tracks Iranian missile launches and US assessments of the arsenal.

Can a Fattah missile be intercepted?

There is no definitive public answer. Hypersonic missiles like the Fattah are designed to be harder to intercept than traditional ballistic missiles because they fly fast and can maneuver. The US and its allies have missile defense systems (Patriot, THAAD, Aegis, Iron Dome and David's Sling in Israel), but there is no confirmed public record of a Fattah being intercepted in combat. Iran claims the Fattah cannot be intercepted; the US has not confirmed or denied a specific interception. The honest answer is: we don't know for certain, because the Fattah has not been tested in a way that produced a public, verified interception. See the missile defense reporting on the dashboard.

Could the US shoot down a hypersonic missile?

Possibly, but it is not a proven capability, and the US has not demonstrated it publicly. The US has missile defense systems designed to counter ballistic missiles, and it is developing systems specifically for hypersonic threats. But hypersonic missiles are a recognized challenge — they fly faster and maneuver more than traditional ballistic missiles, which makes them harder to track and intercept. There is no public, verified record of the US shooting down a hypersonic missile in combat. The US military has said it is working on hypersonic defense, but the capability is not proven in the way traditional missile defense is. The dashboard tracks US missile defense activity in the region.

Does Iran have a S-400 air defence system?

It is not confirmed that Iran has a fully operational S-400 battery. There are reports that Russia has supplied S-400 air defense system components to Iran, but the operational status is not publicly verified. The S-400 is Russia's most advanced long-range air defense system. Reports of deliveries have circulated, but whether the system is installed, crewed, and ready is not publicly verified. Iran has its own air defense systems (including the Bavar-373, which it describes as comparable to the S-400), and it has used them against US drones and aircraft. Whether a Russian S-400 is actually defending Iranian airspace right now is unconfirmed. See the Russia–Iran cooperation reports on the dashboard.

Can Iran detect F-35?

Iran says it can, but there is no public proof. Iran claims it can detect stealth aircraft including the F-35, but there is no verified public evidence that Iran has tracked or engaged an F-35. The F-35 is designed to be very difficult to detect by radar. Iran has radar systems and has claimed to detect US stealth aircraft in the past, but those claims have not been independently verified. The US and Israel have flown F-35s in the region, including in operations against Iran, without a confirmed Iranian interception. The honest answer is: Iran says it can, but there is no public proof. The dashboard tracks verified air operations in the region.

What is the missile range from Iran to Israel?

Iran's missiles easily cover the distance to Israel. Iran is roughly 1,000 to 1,500 kilometers from Israel, depending on the launch point (western Iran is closer; the distance from Tehran to Tel Aviv is about 1,600 km). The Shahab-3 has a range of about 1,300–2,000 km, the Khorramshahr about 2,000 km, and the Fattah about 1,400 km. So Iran can strike Israel with its existing missile arsenal, and it has done so — Iran launched missile attacks on Israel, and the dashboard tracks Iranian missile activity.

Is Russia giving Iran fighter jets?

Reports say Russia has agreed to supply Su-35 fighter jets to Iran, and that some may have been delivered, but the exact number and operational status are not publicly confirmed. The Su-35 is a modern Russian fighter, and it would be a significant upgrade for Iran's air force (which flies older aircraft). Reports of deliveries have circulated since 2023, but there is no verified public confirmation of how many Su-35s Iran has or whether they are operational. The dashboard tracks Russia–Iran military cooperation.

How many missiles does Iran have left?

There is no definitive public number. Iran is believed to have one of the largest missile arsenals in the Middle East — estimates before the current conflict put it in the thousands of ballistic missiles, with the ability to produce more. Iran has fired hundreds of missiles in the current conflict (at Israel, at US forces, and at shipping), and it continues to launch them, which shows the stockpile is large. But the exact number remaining is classified by both sides and cannot be verified from public sources. What we know: Iran has fired a lot of missiles and still has the ability to fire more. The precise inventory is not public. US intelligence assesses Iran retains 1,500–2,500 missiles — a low-confidence estimate tracked on the dashboard.
